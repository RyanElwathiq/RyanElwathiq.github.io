The campaign report arrives at the end of the month: 80,000 reach, 1,500 likes, 45 messages, and "excellent engagement". On paper, the lead generation is working. Yet you look at the numbers feeling something is missing, and you cannot name it.

What is missing is the only question that matters: **which of these numbers paid a bill?**

<Callout title="The short version">
  **Lead** = someone raised their hand and said "interested". **Sale** = money actually came in. **ROAS** = for every dinar spent on ads, how much came back. Every other number (likes, reach, emoji comments) is **vanity**: looks great in the report, feeds no one. A campaign is measured by the first three, stage by stage.
</Callout>

## Lead generation, sales, ROAS: the three numbers that matter

**First: the Lead.** Someone filled a form, sent a message asking about the price, called. They have not paid yet, but they raised their hand and said "I am here". The lead is the raw material of sales, and the most valuable signal an ad can bring.

**Second: the Sale.** Money entered the account. Not "almost certainly will buy". Actually paid. This is the only number that pays salaries and rent, and every other number exists to lead to it.

**Third: ROAS.** The scary acronym with the very simple meaning: spent 400 JD on ads and they brought 1,200 in sales? That is a ROAS of 3. Every dinar returned three. And if every dinar returns 0.6? Your campaign is eating money, and it should stop, not continue.

## The disaster of mixing them

Here is where the real damage happens, quietly:

A campaign that brought **a hundred leads and zero sales** can be read as "failed", so you stop it. The truth? The ad is doing great and the leads are real, but the replies are slow or the offer is weak. The problem is in [the journey](/signals/en-clicks-no-sales/), not the campaign, and you just killed your best asset.

The reverse too: a campaign bringing **thousands of likes and zero leads** reads as "successful", so you raise its budget. Now you are paying more... for decoration.

That is why proper measurement checks **each stage separately**: is the ad bringing leads? Are leads becoming sales? Is ROAS above one? Three questions, each exposing a problem in a different place.
