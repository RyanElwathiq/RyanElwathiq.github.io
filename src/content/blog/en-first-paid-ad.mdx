Almost every week someone asks me the same thing: "I want to run a paid ad, but I have no idea where to start." And most of them have already tried. They pressed the boost button on a post, spent a bit, and came out the other side with nothing they could actually read.

So this is the full guide to running your first paid ad on Facebook, Instagram and Google Ads, written in the language of a business owner instead of the language of ad platforms. I am not holding anything back so you have to come back to me. Everything I know goes in here, in order. And at the end there is the other honest part: when you genuinely need someone to manage this for you, and when you do not.

<Callout title="The short version">
  A boosted post is not a campaign. A real campaign starts from a goal tied to a sale, and it needs measurement working **before** the first penny is spent. ## Can you do any of this for free? An honest answer

A lot of people search for a free way to run paid ads, and the phrase hides two different questions. Only one of them has a yes.

**If you mean advertising without paying the platform:** no. Paid means paid. Anyone telling you there is a free way to run paid ads is either selling you a course or describing organic content and calling it advertising.

**If you mean doing it yourself without paying someone to manage it:** yes, and that is exactly what this guide teaches. Every tool involved is free. Ads Manager, the pixel, the reports, split testing, the analytics. The only thing you pay is the media budget itself. The management fee is the part you save.

And there is a third thing that genuinely costs nothing and matters more than budget size: **organic content as your laboratory**. Post the ideas on your own account first and watch which one stops people and pulls real questions. The post that worked organically is your strongest candidate for an ad, because it was tested before it cost you anything.

## The pixel and measurement: why this matters more than targeting

If you take one thing from this article, take this one.

The platform does not know who your customer is. It learns from the signals it receives. When you install the pixel and define a purchase event, you are saying: these are the people, find me more of them. Fairly quickly it stops looking for people who click and starts looking for people who **buy**. The gap in results between a properly measured account and an unmeasured one is far wider than the gap between clever targeting and ordinary targeting.

There is a second layer worth knowing. A chunk of those events never arrives, because browsers and privacy settings block part of the tracking. That is why platforms now support sending events from your server as well as from the browser. It recovers some of the missing picture, and it means your decisions are based on something closer to reality.

Here is the part that stings: **this takes time.** Installing the tracking properly, testing that events actually fire, and confirming they match reality can eat a full day the first time, and can genuinely need help if your site is built in an awkward way. It is also the single day that saves the most burnt budget, and the first thing I check in any [diagnosis](/brief/) of an ad account.

## Your first budget: where to start

I will not hand you a number, because a number without context is a lie. The rule I work by is this: **the budget has to be big enough to learn from, not big enough to profit from in week one.** Your first budget buys information, not sales. If it is so small that no meaningful data comes out of it, you have spent and learned nothing, which is the worst of the possible outcomes.

The practical rule that follows from it: split that first budget across a few different versions instead of pouring it into one. The same total produces completely different results depending on where you put it, and what the split costs you comes back as information about what actually works for you.

## When your own hands are enough, and when they are not

This is the section most guides avoid, because it does not serve whoever wrote them.

**You are fine on your own when:** you have one clear product or service, a local and contained market, a budget small enough that you can genuinely watch it yourself, and a fixed slot each week to sit with the account. In that situation a professional still adds something, but not enough to justify a management fee on top. Run it yourself, learn from your own account, and keep that knowledge for good, even if you hire someone later.

**You need someone when:** the account spends more than you can supervise, you have several products and audiences and separating them has become a job in itself, your buying cycle is long and needs tracking deeper than a single event, or you have spent two months and still cannot explain why the numbers look the way they do. That last one is the clearest signal of all. **The problem is not that it failed. The problem is that you cannot say why.**

Serious [ad management](/services/paid-ads/) is not someone pressing buttons anyway. It is a weekly decision built on numbers: what to stop, what to scale, what to test next. And if the real problem sits deeper than the campaign, in the offer or the positioning or the audience, that is [strategy](/services/marketing-strategy/) work rather than campaign work, and spending before sorting it out is the more expensive path.

I will say the uncomfortable half out loud: **you may not need me.** If your campaigns are running and your numbers improve month over month, leave them alone and do not change things for the sake of changing them.

## Common questions about running a campaign

### How long before I can judge a campaign?

Not in days, in volume of data. You need enough results for the difference you are seeing to be real rather than chance. The practical rule: do not decide until you have enough results to compare, and in the meantime do not touch the campaign daily.

### Can I advertise on Instagram without a Facebook Page?

You can boost a post from inside the Instagram app, but that is the simple promotion described above. To build a real campaign with objectives, tracking and multiple versions, you need Meta Ads Manager, and that runs through a Business account.

### Should I start with Meta or Google?

Ask one question: do people know they need what you sell and search for it? If yes (repairs, urgent services, a recognised product), start with Google. If no (something new, something people love when they see it but never look for), start with Meta. Many businesses eventually need both, just not at the same time and not on a split budget at the start.

### My ad gets clicks but no sales, what now?

Before blaming the ad, inspect what happened after the click: where the visitor landed, how many steps stand between them and the price, and how long a reply to their question takes. An ad that produced clicks did its job. The problem is usually one station further down.

## The takeaway

Running a paid ad is neither magic nor a difficult science. It is a sequence of small decisions in the right order: measure, choose the objective, make a promise, test, read, decide. Anyone willing to learn it can, and everything you need to start today is in this article.
