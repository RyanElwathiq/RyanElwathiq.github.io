Picture this: you open your company's door in the morning and find **a million people** standing outside. All of them potential customers. All of them ready to hear exactly one sentence from you.

What do you tell them?

If your answer is "we offer the best services at the best prices", I have unpleasant news: the million still walk away. Not because they did not hear you. **Because they heard that exact sentence from every company before you, and it never meant anything.**

<Callout title="The short version">
  People are not waiting for an ad. People want to hear something **about themselves**: their problem, their fear, the question sitting in their head before buying. Content that starts from "we" repels. Content that starts from "you" stops people.
</Callout>

## The problem is called selfish content

There is a kind of content that fails not because it looks bad, but because it is **selfish**. It only talks about its owner:

We offer. We are the best. We have experience. Our services are this and that. Contact us.

Meanwhile the customer stands on the other side asking entirely different questions: what is in this for me? Which problem of mine gets solved? Why should I trust you? Why now? Why you specifically?

The more the content revolves around the company, the less the customer cares. Not out of stubbornness. That is the nature of attention: **people stop at what resembles them, not at what praises itself.**

Notice something about the right column: not one sentence praises anything. Every line names **a problem the customer recognises**, and that is exactly what makes them feel you are talking about them.

## Content is a conversation, not a continuous ad

Here is the idea that changes how you think about content entirely: content is not "presence" or "activity on the page". Content is **the conversation that happens before the sale**.

Good content explains the customer's problem before offering the solution. It exposes mistakes they are currently making. It answers their objection before they voice it. It helps them tell the available solutions apart. And it builds trust that whoever is talking understands, rather than just sells.

When the buying moment arrives, the customer picks whoever made them feel **understood**, not whoever they saw most.

## And what does this have to do with sales?

Everything, directly: the conversation builds trust, and trust is a core factor in [the sales equation](/signals/en-sales-guarantee/) we covered earlier. And customer-first content is the first sign you are dealing with [a growth company, not a package company](/signals/en-package-or-growth/).

So before asking "how many posts per week?", ask the better question: **"what conversation do we want to open with our audience?"**. The post count is a detail. The conversation is the work.
