In the previous two articles we covered [why a package is not a growth plan](/signals/en-package-or-growth/) and [why nobody can guarantee your sales](/signals/en-sales-guarantee/). One practical question remains:

> "Fine. How do I tell, from the first call, whether the company in front of me sells real work or digital decor?"

The good news: there are signs. Even better: they show in the first conversation, before you pay a cent.

<Callout title="The short version">
  The company selling decor never arrives labelled "decor". It arrives with confident, pleasant replies. The three signs: **its first question is about quantity, not your problem** · **it sells you other clients' results as your promise** · **all its talk is about the look, with not a word on message and audience.**
</Callout>

## Sign one: the first question reveals everything

Listen to the first question the company asks. It is the most honest sentence you will ever hear from them.

If it is "how many designs per month?" or "which package suits you?", know that the offer was ready before they knew who you are. The same package pitched to the restaurant, the clinic and the phone shop is now being pitched to you.

If instead it is "what problem are you trying to solve?" or "why do you think people are not buying?", you are facing someone who intends to understand before selling.

The difference is not conversational politeness. Real marketing does not start from design. It starts from understanding the market, the audience, the offer and the weak points. Whoever never asked about them does not plan to work on them.

## Sign two: other people's results presented as your promise

"A restaurant we worked with doubled its sales in two months."

Lovely. But it proves exactly one thing: it happened once, with a different project, in different conditions. Your project differs in everything: budget, audience, offer, market, and the stage your business is at.

Past work is valid as proof of experience, and that is its only job. The moment it is presented as **a guarantee of your result**, it became a sales tool, not evidence. A company that understands says: "these are examples of our work; your result comes out of studying your project."

## Sign three: all the talk is about the look

"Our design quality has no competition. Our videos are cinematic. Your identity will be the most premium in the market."

Not a word about your message. Not a word about your audience. Not a word about why anyone would buy from you.

Good design stops the eye. That is its job and it does it well. But what moves the decision is the message: a real problem, a convincing offer, a reason to trust. A simple design with a strong message beats a luxurious design with no meaning, every time.

So when all the company talks about is the look, it is selling you the visible part of the work, not the part that sells.

## The takeaway, in two lines

Digital decor is not a scam. It is real work sold with the wrong job title. The problem only starts when it is sold as the fix for your sales, which it is not.

So before signing with anyone (me included), listen to the first question they ask you. If it is about you and your problem, keep talking. If it is about the number of designs, you now know exactly what is being sold to you.
