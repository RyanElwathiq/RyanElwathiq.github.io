The question that reaches me most often, in different shapes, is always the same question:

> "If I pay you, what do you guarantee? How much of it comes back as sales?"

And it is a completely fair question. Whoever pays has the right to know what the money buys. The problem is not the question. The problem is the answer most people get.

<Callout title="The short version">
  No respectable marketing company on earth can guarantee your sales before studying your project. Whoever guarantees results on the first call is selling you the sentence you want to hear, not the service you need. **Serious people guarantee the method, not the future.**
</Callout>

## Why can nobody guarantee? Because the ad is not the whole equation

Let me say it plainly: a sale does not happen from the ad alone. The ad does exactly one job, it brings the right person to your door. What happens after the door is what decides:

- **The product itself**: worth the price or not?
- **The offer**: why buy from you and not the shop next door?
- **Trust**: who vouched for you? What do the reviews say?
- **The page or account**: where does the click land? On something that sells, or something that confuses?
- **The reply**: the customer asked. Who answered, and how much later?
- **Competitors**: what is on display around you at that same moment?

Any one of these can kill an excellent campaign single-handedly. And most of them are not even in the marketing company's hands. They are in yours.

One more: even a company with a full in-house marketing team cannot guarantee an absolute result for itself. So how does someone who met you once promise you one?

## Try it yourself: the ad is excellent, the rest is on you

I built a small game that makes the point better than a thousand sentences. Inside it there is an ad that is **excellent and locked**: a guaranteed thousand clicks you cannot touch. All you can move is the rest of the equation.

Try putting everything low and see how many sales come out of a thousand clicks. Then raise one factor at a time. And notice one thing: the equation **multiplies, it does not add**. One dead factor kills the whole result even when everything else is a ten out of ten.

## So what can actually be guaranteed?

This is the difference between someone selling you a promise and someone working with you properly.

What can be guaranteed is **the method**: that your budget is never spent on guessing. Research before execution, small tests before the big budget, numbers read every week, and decisions that change based on what the numbers say. Stop what loses, scale what wins.

That can be guaranteed because it is in the hands of whoever does the work. "I will bring you a hundred customers a month" is a promise about something half outside their hands, in a market they probably have not studied yet.
